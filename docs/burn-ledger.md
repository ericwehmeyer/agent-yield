## The two units disagree, and that is the finding

The same corpus, split by what the tokens actually were. Dollars use
`pricing.py`'s formula term by term -- cache reads at 0.10x a base input token,
cache writes at 1.25x or 2.00x, output at 5.00x.

| component | tokens | share of tokens | dollars | share of dollars |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| cache read | 3,127,698,280 | 97.3% | $1,514.50 | 69.1% |
| input | 293,109 | 0.0% | $1.38 | 0.1% |
| cache write | 79,925,027 | 2.5% | $478.56 | 21.8% |
| output | 8,153,556 | 0.3% | $197.54 | 9.0% |
| **total** | **3,216,069,972** | | **$2,191.99** | |

Cache reads are **97.3% of the tokens and 69.1% of the money**. Output is
**0.3% of the tokens and 9.0% of the money**. A ledger kept in tokens is
very nearly a ledger of cache reads; a ledger kept in dollars is most of the way
to a ledger of what was written. Ranking anything on the token column ranks
cache-hit rate, which is why #72 moved the org dashboard's headline off it.

**The total is a lower bound.** 2 models unpriced (<synthetic>, claude-opus-4-8); 74,888,501 cache-write tokens with no TTL, priced at the 5m default. No rate was invented to close
the gap: `pricing.py` carries only rates reconciled against the CLI's own
`modelUsage.costUSD`, and an unreconciled constant is the thing that module
exists to refuse. #81 solved sonnet-5 and fable-5 that way; #86 is what is
left, and names a `<synthetic>` fixture tag that should not be in a real
corpus at all.

3 calls carry a lower-bound `output_tokens` -- their terminal
record was never written -- so the output row and the total are short by
whatever those calls emitted.

## What the cost bands actually cover

Main-thread calls at or above each limit in `thresholds.py`. The share of the
bill is the number a band is worth acting on; the share of calls is what acting
on it disturbs.

| band | limit | calls | share of main calls | dollars | share of main dollars |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| dispatch | 300,000 | 2,073 | 34.0% | $552.07 | 54.4% |
| restart | 500,000 | 463 | 7.6% | $191.93 | 18.9% |
| stop | 700,000 | 103 | 1.7% | $55.15 | 5.4% |

These are cumulative, not exclusive: every `stop` call is also a `restart` call
and a `dispatch` call.

## What this is not

One operator, one machine, one clone. `#20` is the blind re-run on a second, and
`#66` is the macOS ledger that would make this two machines rather than one.
Dollars are list-price equivalents and not a bill: on a subscription the ranking
of two ways of working survives, the absolute figure does not, and no report may
claim otherwise.

The context-volume argument built on the same corpus is `docs/context-cost.html`,
deliberately kept in tokens because it is a retrospective about how much context
was read rather than a comparison between ways of working.
